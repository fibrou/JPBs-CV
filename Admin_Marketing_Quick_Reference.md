## Interview Talking Points

### When Asked About Leadership Philosophy

*"I build programs that serve students, faculty, and institutions simultaneously. Success comes from strategic vision combined with operational excellence and authentic stakeholder engagement. For example, when we restructured the undergraduate curriculum at Oklahoma, I worked directly with faculty to ensure buy-in while maintaining enrollment goals and institutional revenue targets."*

### When Asked About Handling Challenges

*"At Rutgers, I led the online MBA initiative during a period of budget constraints. Rather than seeing it as a limitation, I saw it as an opportunity to create a flexible, scalable program that could generate revenue while serving working professionals. This required convincing skeptical faculty, working with instructional designers, and managing the technology implementation - but ultimately the program succeeded because we focused on quality and student outcomes."*

### When Asked About Data-Driven Decision Making

*"Every major decision I've made as an administrator has been informed by data. For the MS Finance program, I analyzed enrollment trends, competitor offerings, market demand, and student feedback before redesigning our curriculum and admissions process. The result was 58% enrollment growth and improved retention. I believe data should inform - not dictate - decisions, always balanced with qualitative insights from faculty and students."*

### When Asked About Faculty Development

*"I've recruited faculty at three different institutions - Rutgers, Oklahoma, and Estonian Business School. My approach is to look for scholars who are excellent researchers AND committed teachers. At Oklahoma, I helped junior faculty navigate the tenure process while also creating opportunities for senior faculty to lead program innovations. For example, I paired experienced faculty with newer colleagues to mentor them on our student investment fund course, which became one of our signature offerings."*

## Common Weaknesses to Address Proactively

### Limited Experience as Top Administrator
**Address:** *"While I haven't served as a dean yet, my experience as Assistant Director gave me direct exposure to dean-level decision-making, including managing a unit with 1,050 students, strategic planning, and working with the development office on major gifts."*

### Research vs. Administration Balance
**Address:** *"I've successfully balanced research productivity with administrative responsibilities. Even while directing the MS program, I maintained an active research pipeline with publications in top journals. I see this as a strength - staying current with research makes me a better administrator who understands faculty needs."*

### Moving from Faculty to Administration
**Address:** *"I've been preparing for senior administrative roles through progressively responsible positions: Area Head, Assistant Director, and Program Director. Each role taught me different aspects of academic leadership, and I'm ready to synthesize this experience into a broader administrative position."*
